# Raw data observations

The proposed plots can measure something real, but **the choice of market and snapshot matters as much as the binning**. Much of the Polymarket dump consists of repeated short contracts; many Manifold forecasts are already old at the proposed snapshot. Resolution time and the stored close time also answer different questions, and neither is necessarily the time the answer became public.

## Polymarket

**The full dump is dominated by recurring templates.** Of 408,863 market records, 120,971 have “up or down” in the question. One example is “Ethereum Up or Down - October 4, 5:45AM-6:00AM ET.” A simple digit-replacement grouping finds more than 5,000 records in each of several January crypto templates. These are market counts, not independent events; even unrelated repeated time intervals are a very particular forecasting task. Show a broad baseline and a separate substantive-question sample. Inspect the largest families and group related contracts before interpreting uncertainty. A template is not automatically an event: combining all numbers can also merge unrelated questions.

**Binary outcomes are not necessarily YES/NO.** The dump has 120,960 `Up`/`Down` and 50,455 `Over`/`Under` records, besides 161,993 `Yes`/`No` records. “Mississippi gubernatorial election: Presley (D) vs. Reeves (R)” has candidate names as outcomes. Older records can have more than two outcomes, such as an Elon Musk tweet-count market with five ranges. Read the outcome labels and token arrays in order; do not assume every first token means YES or choose only the winner of a multi-outcome question. Some present-day-looking YES/NO questions are also competing options in the same event.

**Event grouping and timing are limited by available metadata.** The local metadata has question, slug, creation and end dates, volume, outcome names and token IDs, but no event ID, tags, explicit resolution, or closed timestamp. Text can find useful families, but it will miss relationships and merge others. In particular, a recorded `end_date` is not evidence of when the outcome became known or of the original schedule. Do not claim that the files alone give a verified 12-hour pre-resolution forecast. Manually inspect event grouping and difficult timing examples before treating either as ground truth.

**Raw fills require careful interpretation.** In 200 randomly selected trade files containing two million rows, about 41% have one of the two exchange addresses as taker. These are consistent with the exchange-summary pattern described in Gemini's note; this count alone does not prove how many rows duplicate another fill. Check matched transactions before using them for trade counts or volume. Map each token to its outcome, invert the second token when expressing a two-outcome market as the first outcome's probability, and keep execution price distinct from a displayed quote. This sample does not establish whether removing summary rows changes calibration.

## Manifold

**Activity does not guarantee a fresh forecast.** Among 2,940 downloaded markets with a usable non-redemption bet at least one day before resolution, 751 had a latest such bet more than a week before that snapshot; 265 were over a month old. For “Will any other high-level academic or public figure resign or be fired because of plagiarism allegations in 2024?”, the most recent bet was about 316 days old at the snapshot, with probability 82.4%. Other stale examples were already near 0% or 100%, so the suggested pull toward 50% is not established. Record the age of each forecast and compare freshness slices; a freshness requirement changes which markets the plot represents.

**Stored close time is not a reliable substitute for the originally scheduled deadline.** In all 37,680 markets passing the stated YES/NO, volume and bettor filters, resolution time equals or follows the stored close time; it never precedes it. This does not show that no market resolved early: the stored close time may have been changed. For example, “Will Anthropic release a new Claude Fable model before October 26, 2026?” was resolved about 1.4 days after its stored close time. We have not reconstructed the original deadline or the time the answer became known. Compare close-based and resolution-based snapshots, label them accurately, and treat early resolution as unresolved until the original schedule can be established.

**Long horizons select different questions.** Of those 37,680 eligible markets, 30,258 were created less than a year before resolution; they cannot provide a one-year pre-resolution snapshot from their recorded market lifetime. The remaining sample is not comparable to the one-day sample without qualification. Show counts at every horizon and compare horizons on a shared cohort as a secondary analysis. Creation time is only a proxy for the start of available market history.

**Topics and creators help filtering, but are not truth labels.** Among 17,248 downloaded full records, 1,125 have no group slugs. “Will Jannik Sinner play in Rome M1000 2026” is tagged both `ai` and sports; “Will OpenAI run a Super Bowl ad?” legitimately crosses both. Topic filters need a manual audit. The most prolific eligible creator, BTE, has 1,300 markets, including distinct political questions; a creator cap alone would discard diverse questions. Other questions include “Will this market reach 49%?” and “Daily Coinflip,” illustrating self-referential and random markets that need a different filter from sports or crypto.

## Details

All observations are from local files only. Polymarket metadata counts include open and closed records and are not a resolved-market sample. Trade files were sampled uniformly by file with seed 42; each selected file holds a consecutive block of fills, so the two million rows are clustered, not independent random trades. Price endpoint and calibration effects were not tested.

The Manifold lite file contained 107,761 records, including cancellations and fractional `MKT` resolutions; the eligible set requires YES/NO, at least 15 lifetime unique bettors and at least 1,000 lifetime mana volume. Those thresholds use information from after earlier snapshots and can cause selection bias. Full records and histories are an in-progress, non-random downloaded subset. For the staleness check, 3,000 existing history files were selected with seed 42; the snapshot is resolution time minus one day, with the latest non-redemption record having `probAfter` and `createdTime` no later than that snapshot. This is a preliminary reconstruction: limit-order execution timing and whether each record's probability is appropriate for carry-forward need further checking. Five markets with a long enough lifetime had no prior usable record; shorter-lived markets were also excluded. Final stored close times are not known to be original scheduled close times.
