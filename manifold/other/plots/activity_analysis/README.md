# Activity, initial prices, and the snapshot pipeline

**More trading does not make the downward gap disappear.** One week after creation, status-quo-NO world markets with at least 15 recorded bettors average 38% forecast and 30% YES. With at least 100 bettors, the figures are 40% and 34%, but these are different, much fewer markets. The same-market comparison also remains below the diagonal. High thresholds and individual bins can be noisy; these comparisons do not establish that activity caused any change. Related questions can share outcomes, and these plots do not show uncertainty intervals.

About 98% of one-week markets started at 50%, estimated from `probBefore` of the first price-moving bet. Yet only about 1% of snapshot prices remained within 0.5 percentage points of that initial price. One-week status-quo markets priced at least 50% averaged 74% but resolved YES 65%. A simple pull toward 50% predicts the opposite sign above 50%, so it cannot by itself explain the downward gap. This does not rule out more complicated effects of initial prices. The last price move was at least a day old for roughly 45% of one-week snapshots; old does not necessarily mean wrong.

**A confirmed count error barely changes the headline.** Zero-amount unfilled orders were counted as bettors. After removing them, the one-week status-quo sample falls from 6,242 to 6,137; both percentages above round to the same values. The snapshot code is fixed, but the existing `data/manifold/snapshots.parquet` and original figures are unchanged. Rebuild them before using the fix elsewhere. The red-labelled comparison shows both versions.

**Price timing is unresolved.** In a seeded sample of 500 one-week world snapshots with at least 15 original bettors, 28 had an earlier price-moving order with a nonzero fill after the snapshot; in 5 the last selected price-moving order did. Orders can contain both immediate and later fills. The code uses the order's creation time and final stored `probAfter`; the raw fields alone do not establish whether that price includes later fills. This is a concern, not proof that all five prices are wrong. An unambiguous reconstruction needs further investigation. The audit also found 118 sampled markets with at least one extra zero-only user. No comprehensive fill or API completeness audit was done.

## Details and reproduction

- [Bettors](bettors.png) and [volume](volume.png) compare three thresholds at 1 day, 1 week and 30 days after creation, separately for world markets classified as status-quo NO and other world markets. Snapshot volume sums absolute fill amounts through that time, falling back to order amount when fills are absent; it may count both sides of a match and is not verified as Manifold's own volume measure. Every curve also requires 15 original counted bettors by the snapshot. Thresholds select different markets. Plotted points are the mean forecast and YES fraction within the original price bins, omitting bins with fewer than 10 markets; lines connect remaining points.
- [Same cohort](same_cohort.png) uses markets present and meeting the original 15-bettor threshold at all three horizons. Requiring survival through a month and early activity selects longer-lived, more active markets. A market may move between probability bins. [Before and after correction](bettor_count_before_after.png) shows the original and fixed 15-bettor counts in each panel, using the same bins. All other activity plots use the original counts; the difference from the correction is small in the separate comparison, not assumed zero.
- All figures use only previously fetched, resolved YES/NO binary markets with at least 15 lifetime bettors and 1,000 lifetime platform volume; this selection uses future information. Markets unresolved at data collection are absent, disproportionately excluding longer and newer questions. Each after-creation snapshot also requires the market to remain unresolved at that time. Selection by resolution status and survival can affect results. The original fetched set contains many more older resolved markets than recent ones; `selection.log` records counts by creation year.
- Run `.venv/bin/python manifold/other/activity_analysis.py`, `manifold/other/activity_audit.py`, and `manifold/other/activity_correction.py` from the project root, with the latter two also invoked using `.venv/bin/python`. The initial activity analysis reads the original saved parquet; the correction script recomputes counts from raw JSON without overwriting it. `metrics.csv`, `bins.csv`, `count_correction.csv`, `audit_sample.csv`, and `initial_checks.csv` contain the underlying numbers. `audit_examples.json` records examples. The raw bets are expected in API ascending order; the initial-price analysis asserts that ordering, but equal-time ordering and API completeness remain uncertain.
